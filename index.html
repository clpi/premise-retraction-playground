Contrast: a design that is not exact

Gradient descent records one update per example, computed from weights that earlier examples had already moved. Subtracting a recorded update afterwards does not reproduce the model trained without that example.

How this works

the whole argument, in four paragraphs

1 · The model

One input x, one output y, two weights: bias b and slope w, with ŷ(x) = b + w·x. The objective is least squares, L = Σᵢ cᵢ(ŷ(xᵢ) − yᵢ)². Zeroing the derivative gives Aθ = q, where A = Σᵢ cᵢx̃ᵢx̃ᵢᵀ and q = Σᵢ cᵢyᵢx̃ᵢ, with x̃ᵢ = (1, xᵢ). The weights are therefore a function of a sum of per-premise terms — nothing else.

2 · The retraction mechanism

Each premise stores exactly its own 2×2 block Aᵢ and its own 2-vector qᵢ — open a ledger row to see them. Adding a premise adds its block to the running totals; retracting it subtracts that block and nothing else, then re-solves. Restore adds it back. There is no replay, no re-shuffle, no retraining job.

3 · Why the check cannot fail

Every number is an exact rational (BigInt numerator and denominator), so a subtraction leaves no residue. The check then compares two different paths: the incrementally maintained totals with one term subtracted, against totals rebuilt by summing only the remaining premises from zero. It compares b, w and every prediction as exact rationals, confirms the removed term re-adds to the previous totals, and confirms the surviving contributions are byte-identical. Floats appear only as pixel coordinates on the chart.

4 · What is real, what is illustrative

Everything in the panels is computed live in your browser: real rational arithmetic, a real closed-form solve, a real check. Nothing is replayed from a recording. The demo premises (readings A, B, C) are arbitrary teaching data, not measurements of anything. Scope is two weights on purpose: the claim being demonstrated is the accounting, not the capacity.

Exact arithmetic: BigInt rationals. No libraries, no build step, no network calls, no storage. Premise retraction playground · a miniature of Idol's premise-tracked learning
